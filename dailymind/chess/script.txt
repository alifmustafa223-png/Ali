The queen used to be the weakest piece in chess! She could only move one square. So what changed?

Chess was born in India, about fifteen hundred years ago. It was a game of war, called chaturanga.

The pieces were a real army. Elephants, horses, chariots, and foot soldiers.

In Persia, when the king was trapped, players said "shah mat." The king is helpless. That's where checkmate comes from!

And back then, there was no queen. Next to the king stood his advisor, crawling one square at a time.

When chess reached Europe, the advisor became a queen. But she was still the weakest piece.

Then, in Spain, in the late fourteen hundreds, everything changed. The queen could suddenly fly across the whole board.

Some historians think a real ruler inspired it. Queen Isabella of Spain.

Players called it mad queen chess. Games got faster, and way more exciting.

And it stuck. Today, the queen is the strongest piece on the board.
