This tower should have collapsed eight hundred years ago... and a war saved it.

Pisa, eleven-seventy-three. Builders start a bell tower on soft, wet sand.

Five years later, only three floors up... it starts to lean. Badly.

Then war breaks out. Work stops for almost a hundred years.

And that pause saved it. Slowly, the soil packed down under all that weight.

When builders returned, they tried a trick. Make the new floors taller on the short side.

So the tower isn't just leaning... it's curved. Like a banana.

By nineteen-ninety, the top hung four and a half meters out. They closed it.

Engineers stacked six hundred tons of lead on one side... then pulled soil out from under the other, bucket by bucket.

It worked. The tower straightened by forty-five centimeters... and it's safe for at least two hundred more years.
