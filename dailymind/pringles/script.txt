This man is buried... inside a can of chips. And it was his own idea. His name was Fred Baur: a Navy veteran with a Ph.D. in chemistry, working at Procter and Gamble. In the nineteen sixties, chips came in bags: half air, full of broken crumbs. Fred had a strange plan. Make every chip the same curved shape, so they could stack, one on top of another, like tiny roof tiles. Then he designed a tall tube to hold the stack. No air. No crushed chips. In nineteen seventy, he got the patent, and that tube became one of the most famous snack cans on Earth. But here's the twist. Fred was so proud of that can, he told his family: when I die, put me in it. In two thousand eight, he died at eighty-nine. And his kids kept their promise.
