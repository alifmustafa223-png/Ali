What if every time someone thought about you, you got one dollar? Not when they text you. Not when they like your post. Just... when they think about you.

By the end of this week, you would be a millionaire. And by the end of the month, you would be begging for it to stop.

Let's find out why.

It's Monday morning. Your phone buzzes. It's a notification from your bank. Plus one dollar. Then another. Plus one. Plus one.

There's no name on the payment. It just says: "Somebody thought of you."

You check the time. It's six forty-two in the morning. Downstairs, your mom is making breakfast, wondering if you're going to be late again. Plus one dollar.

At school, your best friend saves you a seat. Plus one. Your teacher looks at your empty homework folder. Plus one. Actually... plus three.

By lunch, you've made eighteen dollars. Not bad for doing absolutely nothing.

And then you notice something. Your crush is sitting two tables away, laughing with their friends. You look down at your phone. Zero dollars. Ouch.

Then they glance your way. Your phone buzzes. Plus one dollar! Your heart jumps. And then you look down and see the giant ketchup stain on your shirt. Right. That's why.

So you start running tests. Because if this is real, you need to know exactly how it works.

Rule number one. It doesn't matter if the thought is nice. When your little brother finds out you ate the last of his cereal, he's furious. Plus one dollar. Plus one. Plus one. You still get paid.

Rule number two. It counts every single time. If someone thinks about you ten times, that's ten dollars.

Rule number three. And this one is weird. It works at night too. At two thirteen in the morning, your phone lights up. Plus one dollar. Somebody, somewhere, is lying awake thinking about you.

You decide not to ask who.

Rule number four. You can't pay yourself. You stand in front of the mirror and think about yourself as hard as you possibly can. You think about how great your hair looks. You think about your best qualities. Nothing. Zero dollars. Apparently, the universe has rules against that.

By the end of day one, you've made forty-seven dollars. Most of it from your mom.

That night, you can't sleep. So you build a tiny app. Every time a dollar comes in, it puts a dot on a map, showing where the thought came from.

Your house is covered in dots. Your school has a few. Your grandma's house, three hours away, has one big glowing dot. She thinks about you every night before bed. You make a note to call her more often.

Now, let's do some math.

A normal person is thought about maybe fifty times a day. Your family, your friends, your teachers, that one kid you bumped into in the hallway. That's fifty dollars a day. About eighteen thousand dollars a year, just for existing.

But what about someone famous?

A popular YouTuber with ten million fans might be thought about millions of times every single day. That's millions of dollars. Every. Single. Day.

And the biggest celebrities on Earth? Hundreds of millions of thoughts. They would become the richest people who ever lived in less than a week.

And here's the crazy part. It wouldn't even stop when you die. Think about Shakespeare. Every year, millions of students are forced to think about him in English class. He'd still be getting paid. Hundreds of years later.

And that's when it clicks. The money isn't in being liked. The money is in being noticed.

Tuesday. You make a plan.

First, you try the easy stuff. You dye your hair bright blue. People stare all day. Plus sixty dollars.

Then you stand on a lunch table and sing the national anthem. Loudly. And badly. Plus three hundred dollars. And a week of detention. Which means the principal is now thinking about you too. Plus one.

It's working. But it's not enough. If you want real money, you need the whole internet.

You film yourself doing the dumbest thing you can think of. You fill your entire bedroom with one thousand rubber ducks. Then you jump into them. And you post it with a two-word caption: "Why not?"

At first, nothing happens. Twelve views. Plus twelve dollars.

Then a huge account shares it.

Plus one hundred. Plus one thousand. Plus ten thousand. Your phone gets so hot you have to put it in the fridge.

By midnight, two million people have seen the duck video. And every single one of them thought about you for at least a second.

You go to bed with two million dollars. You wake up with five.
