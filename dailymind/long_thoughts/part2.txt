Wednesday is a blur.

You pay off your parents' house. You buy your best friend the gaming computer they always wanted. You order every pizza in town and give them away for free. Which, by the way, makes the whole town think about you. More money.

Then you try to buy a sports car. The salesman laughs. You're fifteen. You can't even drive. But you leave with a very nice golf cart, and the salesman thinks about you for the rest of the day.

News reporters start showing up outside your house. "Who is the duck kid?" Every time the story airs, your phone vibrates like crazy.

But then the bank calls. And calls again. A teenager getting millions of dollars from nowhere? That looks suspicious. They freeze your account.

So you hire a lawyer. A very expensive lawyer, who can't stop thinking about how strange your case is. Plus one dollar, every few minutes. Even your problems are paying you now.

And then Thursday happens.

Someone posts a video saying your duck video was fake. That you paid people to share it. Thousands of people get angry.

And here's the thing about angry people. They think about you way more than happy people do. They think about you in the shower. They think about you while they type mean comments. They think about you right before they fall asleep.

Your money doesn't drop. It explodes. Ten million dollars, in one afternoon.

You open your thought map. Before, it was full of warm little dots around your home. Now the whole planet is glowing. Every city, every country. Millions of people, all thinking about you at the same time. And almost none of them are thinking anything nice.

And now you face the hardest question of your life. If people hating you makes you richer... would you let them?

At first, you say no. Of course not.

But the numbers are right there, glowing on your screen. And every time you do something annoying, the counter spins faster.

So you start picking fights online. You make reaction videos where you roast everyone. You become the person the whole internet loves to hate.

You start fake drama with a famous singer. Her fans flood your comments. Millions of angry messages. Millions of dollars.

At school, people whisper when you walk by. Your little brother asks why everyone is being mean about you online. You tell him it's just a game. But he doesn't look convinced.

And your mom? She doesn't say anything. She just looks at you a little differently at dinner.

By Friday, you are a billionaire.

And by Friday, your best friend has stopped answering your texts.

Here's what nobody tells you about being famous on the internet. People move on. Fast.

Saturday morning, somebody else goes viral. A guy who taught his cat to play the drums. And just like that, the whole world forgets about you.

Ten million thoughts a day becomes one million. Then a hundred thousand. Then almost nothing.

You still have the money. But your phone goes quiet. And for the first time all week, nobody is thinking about you.

So you panic. You try to win them back. You fill your house with ten thousand rubber ducks this time. Forty views. You try another fight with the singer. She doesn't even reply.

You stare at the counter for hours, waiting for it to move. It's the loneliest feeling in the world. Not because you're poor. Because nobody's thinking about you anymore.

Except... at six forty-two in the morning, your phone buzzes. Plus one dollar.

It's your mom. She's wondering if you've had breakfast.

Sunday. You sit on the floor of your huge, empty new house. The counter says one point two billion dollars.

And you realize the strangest thing. The billions from strangers felt like nothing. But that one dollar from your mom, every single morning? That's the one that made you smile.

So you decide to do something different with all that money. You build a new library at your school. You pay for your teacher's dream trip. You send your grandma flowers every single week.

And slowly, something changes. The thoughts start coming back. Not millions of them. Just a few. But this time, they're warm. People aren't thinking about the duck kid, or the villain. They're thinking about you.

So you call your best friend. You say sorry. And after a long pause, they say: "It's okay. Honestly... I've been thinking about you all week."

Your phone buzzes. Plus one dollar.

Some people are worth billions. But some thoughts are worth more than money.
