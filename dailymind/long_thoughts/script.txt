What if every time someone thought about you, you got one dollar? Not when they text you. Not when they like your post. Just... when they think about you.

By the end of this week, you would be a millionaire. And by the end of the month, you would be begging for it to stop.

Let's find out why.

It's Monday morning. Your phone buzzes. It's a notification from your bank. Plus one dollar. Then another. Plus one. Plus one.

There's no name on the payment. It just says: "Somebody thought of you."

You check the time. It's six forty-two in the morning. Downstairs, your mom is making breakfast, wondering if you're going to be late again. Plus one dollar.

At school, your best friend saves you a seat. Plus one. Your teacher looks at your empty homework folder. Plus one. Actually... plus three.

By lunch, you've made eighteen dollars. Not bad for doing absolutely nothing.

And then you notice something. Your crush is sitting two tables away, laughing with their friends. You look down at your phone. Zero dollars. Ouch.

Then they glance your way. Your phone buzzes. Plus one dollar! Your heart jumps. And then you look down and see the giant ketchup stain on your shirt. Right. That's why.

So you start running tests. Because if this is real, you need to know exactly how it works.

Rule number one. It doesn't matter if the thought is nice. When your little brother finds out you ate the last of his cereal, he's furious. Plus one dollar. Plus one. Plus one. You still get paid.

Rule number two. It counts every single time. If someone thinks about you ten times, that's ten dollars.

Rule number three. And this one is weird. It works at night too. At two thirteen in the morning, your phone lights up. Plus one dollar. Somebody, somewhere, is lying awake thinking about you.

You decide not to ask who.

Rule number four. You can't pay yourself. You stand in front of the mirror and think about yourself as hard as you possibly can. You think about how great your hair looks. You think about your best qualities. Nothing. Zero dollars. Apparently, the universe has rules against that.

By the end of day one, you've made forty-seven dollars. Most of it from your mom.

That night, you can't sleep. So you build a tiny app. Every time a dollar comes in, it puts a dot on a map, showing where the thought came from.

Your house is covered in dots. Your school has a few. Your grandma's house, three hours away, has one big glowing dot. She thinks about you every night before bed. You make a note to call her more often.

Now, let's do some math.

A normal person is thought about maybe fifty times a day. Your family, your friends, your teachers, that one kid you bumped into in the hallway. That's fifty dollars a day. About eighteen thousand dollars a year, just for existing.

But what about someone famous?

A popular YouTuber with ten million fans might be thought about millions of times every single day. That's millions of dollars. Every. Single. Day.

And the biggest celebrities on Earth? Hundreds of millions of thoughts. They would become the richest people who ever lived in less than a week.

And here's the crazy part. It wouldn't even stop when you die. Think about Shakespeare. Every year, millions of students are forced to think about him in English class. He'd still be getting paid. Hundreds of years later.

And that's when it clicks. The money isn't in being liked. The money is in being noticed.

Tuesday. You make a plan.

First, you try the easy stuff. You dye your hair bright blue. People stare all day. Plus sixty dollars.

Then you stand on a lunch table and sing the national anthem. Loudly. And badly. Plus three hundred dollars. And a week of detention. Which means the principal is now thinking about you too. Plus one.

It's working. But it's not enough. If you want real money, you need the whole internet.

You film yourself doing the dumbest thing you can think of. You fill your entire bedroom with one thousand rubber ducks. Then you jump into them. And you post it with a two-word caption: "Why not?"

At first, nothing happens. Twelve views. Plus twelve dollars.

Then a huge account shares it.

Plus one hundred. Plus one thousand. Plus ten thousand. Your phone gets so hot you have to put it in the fridge.

By midnight, two million people have seen the duck video. And every single one of them thought about you for at least a second.

You go to bed with two million dollars. You wake up with five.

Wednesday is a blur.

You pay off your parents' house. You buy your best friend the gaming computer they always wanted. You order every pizza in town and give them away for free. Which, by the way, makes the whole town think about you. More money.

Then you try to buy a sports car. The salesman laughs. You're fifteen. You can't even drive. But you leave with a very nice golf cart, and the salesman thinks about you for the rest of the day.

News reporters start showing up outside your house. "Who is the duck kid?" Every time the story airs, your phone vibrates like crazy.

But then the bank calls. And calls again. A teenager getting millions of dollars from nowhere? That looks suspicious. They freeze your account.

So you hire a lawyer. A very expensive lawyer, who can't stop thinking about how strange your case is. Plus one dollar, every few minutes. Even your problems are paying you now.

And then Thursday happens.

Someone posts a video saying your duck video was fake. That you paid people to share it. Thousands of people get angry.

And here's the thing about angry people. They think about you way more than happy people do. They think about you in the shower. They think about you while they type mean comments. They think about you right before they fall asleep.

Your money doesn't drop. It explodes. Ten million dollars, in one afternoon.

You open your thought map. Before, it was full of warm little dots around your home. Now the whole planet is glowing. Every city, every country. Millions of people, all thinking about you at the same time. And almost none of them are thinking anything nice.

And now you face the hardest question of your life. If people hating you makes you richer... would you let them?

At first, you say no. Of course not.

But the numbers are right there, glowing on your screen. And every time you do something annoying, the counter spins faster.

So you start picking fights online. You make reaction videos where you roast everyone. You become the person the whole internet loves to hate.

You start fake drama with a famous singer. Her fans flood your comments. Millions of angry messages. Millions of dollars.

At school, people whisper when you walk by. Your little brother asks why everyone is being mean about you online. You tell him it's just a game. But he doesn't look convinced.

And your mom? She doesn't say anything. She just looks at you a little differently at dinner.

By Friday, you are a billionaire.

And by Friday, your best friend has stopped answering your texts.

Here's what nobody tells you about being famous on the internet. People move on. Fast.

Saturday morning, somebody else goes viral. A guy who taught his cat to play the drums. And just like that, the whole world forgets about you.

Ten million thoughts a day becomes one million. Then a hundred thousand. Then almost nothing.

You still have the money. But your phone goes quiet. And for the first time all week, nobody is thinking about you.

So you panic. You try to win them back. You fill your house with ten thousand rubber ducks this time. Forty views. You try another fight with the singer. She doesn't even reply.

You stare at the counter for hours, waiting for it to move. It's the loneliest feeling in the world. Not because you're poor. Because nobody's thinking about you anymore.

Except... at six forty-two in the morning, your phone buzzes. Plus one dollar.

It's your mom. She's wondering if you've had breakfast.

Sunday. You sit on the floor of your huge, empty new house. The counter says one point two billion dollars.

And you realize the strangest thing. The billions from strangers felt like nothing. But that one dollar from your mom, every single morning? That's the one that made you smile.

So you decide to do something different with all that money. You build a new library at your school. You pay for your teacher's dream trip. You send your grandma flowers every single week.

And slowly, something changes. The thoughts start coming back. Not millions of them. Just a few. But this time, they're warm. People aren't thinking about the duck kid, or the villain. They're thinking about you.

So you call your best friend. You say sorry. And after a long pause, they say: "It's okay. Honestly... I've been thinking about you all week."

Your phone buzzes. Plus one dollar.

Some people are worth billions. But some thoughts are worth more than money.
