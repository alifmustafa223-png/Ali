Airplane windows are round because of a terrible mistake.

In 1952, Britain launched the world's first jet airliner: the Comet. Fast, quiet, the future of flying. Passengers loved it.

And it had square windows.

But in 1954, two Comets broke apart in mid-air, just months apart. Every Comet was grounded, and nobody knew why.

So engineers put an entire plane inside a giant water tank... and pumped it up and down, again and again, like thousands of flights, day and night.

Then it happened. A crack, right at the corner of a window.

Every flight, the cabin is pressurized, and it stretches like a balloon.

A sharp corner focuses all that stress into one tiny point. The metal gets tired... and tears.

A round corner spreads the stress evenly.

That's why every plane window you've ever looked through is rounded.
